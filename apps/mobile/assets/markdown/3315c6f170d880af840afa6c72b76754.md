## Setting up
1. Pick up a pair of kettlebells and stand upright in a near hip-width stance while holding the kettlebells at your sides.
2. While letting your knees naturally bend and keeping your back flat, sit your buttocks back to hinge at your hips and incline your torso as close to horizontal as your flexibility permits.
    1. As a cue for hinging at your hips, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks while your hands are carrying something. Focusing on “sitting back” rather than “bending over” can help you avoid rounding your lower back
    2. Most of the motion should come from your hips, and your shins should remain mostly vertical, but do not keep your knees fully straight. You do not need to focus on bending your knees, since the appropriate degree of knee bend can naturally occur when you sit your buttocks backward without intentionally keeping your knees locked.
3. As your arms hang down toward the ground with the kettlebells vertically under your shoulders, point your palms in toward each other if they are not already facing this direction.
## Exercise technique
1. While letting your elbows bend slightly, sweep your arms out and away from your sides in a wide, arcing motion until your elbows are near shoulder height.
    1. Statically maintain your hinged torso position throughout the set as you avoid moving your lower body or lower back.
    2. The appropriately slight degree of elbow bend can naturally occur if you do not actively try to keep them fully locked. If you feel like you need to bend your elbows to a greater degree to lift the kettlebells up in a row-like motion, you likely selected a weight that is excessively heavy for this variation.
    3. As you move your arms outward and upward, avoid pulling them back toward your hips.
    4. Let the handles rotate in your hands to keep the base of each kettlebell facing the ground.
    5. At the top of the raise, your palms should be directed down toward the ground with mostly straight wrists.
2. In a controlled arcing manner, reverse the motion by moving your arms downward and in toward each other until they are hanging down toward the ground.
    1. While you do not need to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, avoid letting the weights abruptly swing your arms downward. The weights should not bang against each other in the bottom position.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the kettlebells down.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your [rear delt](../Muscles/Deltoid%20Posterior%20Head%201905c6f170d880a29e5ec26f4d257c01.md), which is assisted by your [traps](../Muscles/Trapezius%201b95c6f170d880bebf89c759b592e81f.md) and [rhomboids](../Muscles/Rhomboids%201b95c6f170d880c8a84de94f5232a509.md), along with your rotator cuff’s [infraspinatus](../Muscles/Infraspinatus%201905c6f170d88026aebcf3953e2e440f.md), [teres minor](../Muscles/Teres%20Minor%201905c6f170d88098824dc8136557c410.md), and [supraspinatus](../Muscles/Supraspinatus%201905c6f170d880efb440c1614caf1c97.md).

## Setting up
1. Position a short looped elastic band around both of your lower thighs.
    1. The band will provide the greatest resistance to your hip muscles if you place it just above your knees, while it will provide less resistance if you place it higher up your thighs.
    2. You can choose specifically where you situate the band on your thighs based on the level of resistance you wish to experience. If you are using a particular band for this exercise for the first time, you can adjust its placement during the set if you wish to change the difficulty after starting the set.
    3. Inspect the band to confirm it does not have any small tears, since a small tear can swiftly become a full tear when stretching a band.
2. Stand upright with your feet near shoulder width apart from each other, as your toes are pointing somewhat out to the sides rather than pointing straight ahead.
    1. While people may commonly find that setting up reasonably close to this stance allows them to squat effectively, the specific stance width and degree of toe flare that is ideal will vary among different individuals based on their hip structure and mobility.
    2. Ultimately, you want to position your feet in a manner that results in you feeling comfortable and stable when squatting, which typically requires trying out different stances over time and assessing how you feel with them.
    3. Some people may feel most comfortable squatting with their feet close to hip width apart as their toes are pointing straight ahead, while others will feel best with their feet outside of shoulder width with their toes rotated out to the sides at 45-degree angles.
3. Raise your arms out in front of you until they are approximately parallel to the ground.
    1. You can alternatively keep your arms in a different position if you wish to do so, but keeping your arms outstretched throughout the set provides a counterbalance that often allows people to reach a greater squat depth.
## Exercise technique
1. Initiate the descent by starting to sit your buttocks back. Progressively bend at your knees and hips to squat down, while driving your knees out against the band to keep your knees stacked over your feet.
    1. Let your torso naturally lean forward at your hips to maintain your balance, but keep your back flat and chest up, as you avoid rounding your lower back or letting your chest cave down through upper back rounding.
    2. Keep the entire sole of each foot (including the heel, outer aspect of the midfoot, and inner aspect of the midfoot) firmly planted on the ground as your weight is evenly distributed throughout your feet (rather than being all on your heels or toes) during your set.
    3. As you bend your knees, it is normal for them to track forward over your feet (especially the deeper your squat) to help you maintain your balance.
    4. Strive to keep each knee aligned with the second toe (the toe next to your big toe) or the third toe of that side’s foot as you avoid letting the band pull your knees inward past this point.
2. Continue to squat down through the available range of motion that your mobility enables without rounding your back, lifting your heels up off the ground, or letting your knees cave inward.
    1. People can often descend at least until their thighs are fairly close to being parallel to the ground, but what constitutes a full range of motion for a squat will vary among different individuals.
    2. If your back starts rounding, heels lift off the ground, or knees cave inward at the bottom of your squat, you may be squatting excessively deep relative to your current level of mobility and body control.
3. Reverse the movement by progressively standing up as you drive through the ground while extending (straightening) your knees and hips until you have returned to a fully upright position.
    1. Continue keeping your chest up, back flat, feet fully on the ground, and knees stacked over your feet as you squat up.
4. Alternate between steps 1-3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
While statically training your [gluteus medius](../Muscles/Gluteus%20Medius%201925c6f170d880f38e56e316eab6e33b.md), [gluteus minimus](../Muscles/Gluteus%20Minimus%201925c6f170d880468a1ac852b36eeaf8.md), and [short hip external rotator muscles](../Muscles/Short%20Hip%20External%20Rotators%201b95c6f170d880c79035ce63a7c5f540.md), this exercise dynamically trains your [gluteus maximus](../Muscles/Gluteus%20Maximus%201bb5c6f170d8805380e7c611cd051f9f.md), [adductor magnus](../Muscles/Adductor%20Magnus%201b95c6f170d880518436f037bd6a822d.md), and [quads](../Muscles/Monoarticular%20Quadriceps%20Femoris%201be5c6f170d880119155c2e8184c0d0d.md).

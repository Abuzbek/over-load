## Setting up
1. Add weight plates of your desired load to a barbell situated on the ground.
    1. To set up for this row, you can perform the lifting phase of a [conventional deadlift](Conventional%20deadlift%201a35c6f170d880f7a755cd3780a63371.md) with a rather wide grip, followed by the lowering phase of a [Romanian deadlift](Barbell%20Romanian%20deadlift%201a35c6f170d880bebc64f4dce0d5f5f1.md).
2. In a near hip-width stance, stand with the middle of your feet under the middle of the bar.
3. Sit your hips backward while bending your knees until you can firmly grasp the bar with a well outside shoulder-width overhand grip. Keep your lower back flat and chest up throughout the exercise.
    1. It is generally appropriate to use a grip width that is approximately the distance between your elbows when your arms are raised straight out to your sides, parallel to the ground. The right grip for you should make flaring your elbows out from your sides during the row feel natural and comfortable.
    2. More motion should come from your hips than your knees as you descend, and your knees should not track particularly far forward, but you can allow your knees to shift slightly forward rather than maintaining completely vertical shins.
    3. Throughout the exercise, keep your neck near a neutral position, as when you are looking straight ahead while standing upright.
4. Brace your core by tensing the front and sides of your abdominals outward, like you would if you were bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
    1. Continue keeping your core braced from this point on.
5. Stand upright with the bar by driving through your legs and thrusting your hips forward.
6. While allowing your knees to bend mildly and keeping your back flat, sit your buttocks back to hinge at your hips and incline your torso as close to horizontal as your flexibility permits.
    1. To avoid rounding your lower back, you should think about sitting back rather than bending over.
    2. As a cue for hinging at your hips, you can think about closing a car door with your buttocks while your hands are carrying something.
    3. Most of the motion should come from your hips, and your shins should remain mostly vertical throughout your set, but do not keep your knees fully straight. You do not need to focus on bending your knees, since the appropriately mild degree of knee bend can naturally occur when you sit your buttocks backward without intentionally keeping your knees locked.
    4. Lifters with normal flexibility can typically lower the bar at least below their knees, but how deeply you can hinge without rounding your lower back or excessively pushing your knees forward depends on your flexibility.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull the bar up toward your chest by driving your elbows upward and pulling your shoulder blades together.
    1. Keep your torso fixed in a hinged position as you maintain the same angle of bend in your hips and knees throughout your set while keeping your core braced and your lower back flat.
    2. Movement should occur at your shoulders, shoulder blades, and elbows, but your lower body should remain essentially stationary.
    3. While you do not need to touch your chest with the bar, you should finish with the bar close to reaching it, in contrast to a typical row, where you pull the bar lower down toward your abdomen.
    4. When viewed from behind, your upper arms and torso form close to a “T-shape” at the top of the row with flared elbows, in contrast to a standard row with a narrower grip that finishes with the elbows tucked closer to the sides.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the bar in a controlled manner. Let your elbows move downward and your shoulder blades move apart from each other until you have returned to the starting position with straight elbows.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the bar down onto the floor by bending your knees further.
## Description
This row effectively develops your [rear delts](../Muscles/Deltoid%20Posterior%20Head%201905c6f170d880a29e5ec26f4d257c01.md), [traps](../Muscles/Trapezius%201b95c6f170d880bebf89c759b592e81f.md), and [rhomboids](../Muscles/Rhomboids%201b95c6f170d880c8a84de94f5232a509.md) while also training your [biceps](../Muscles/Biceps%20Brachii%201b95c6f170d88027b895ed4c0bb40b68.md), [brachialis](../Muscles/Brachialis%2018f5c6f170d880c181eed18b5b379fe9.md), [brachioradialis](../Muscles/Brachioradialis%201905c6f170d8808f8207e301c4acd3b3.md), and three [rotator cuff](../Muscles/Rotator%20Cuff%201b95c6f170d880b3b0d8eab69c3a7683.md) muscles.

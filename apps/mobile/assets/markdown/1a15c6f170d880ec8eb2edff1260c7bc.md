## Setting up
1. Symmetrically elevate each end of a mobile flat bench on top of sturdy objects like aerobic steps with risers, bumper plates, plyometric boxes, or deadlift blocks. Elevate it to the level where you can lie face-down on the bench without your hands contacting the ground when your arms are hanging down on each side of the bench.
    1. If you have access to one, you can alternatively use a specific seal row bench, which is already elevated, but this is not a particularly common type of equipment.
    2. When elevating the bench, make sure that each base is level and that you stack the bench onto only objects that are able to handle the combined weight of you, your dumbbells, and the bench.
    3. Setting up this exercise can take more time than most row variations, and you can train your back muscles in a very similar manner with a shorter setup if you perform the “[low incline neutral grip chest-supported dumbbell row](Chest-supported%20elbows-in%20dumbbell%20row%201a15c6f170d8802d969ae139f42062fa.md)” from this database.
2. Place a pair of dumbbells on an elevated surface near the head of your bench in a position where you can grasp them while lying down on the bench.
    1. If there is sufficient space, you would ideally place the dumbbells onto the object you used to elevate the bench.
    2. Alternatively, you can situate the dumbbells on top of bumper plates (or something similar) that you place near the head of the bench in a position you can grasp while lying on the bench, but would not restrict your rowing motion.
    3. With a sufficiently low setup, you also may be able to position the dumbbells on the floor underneath the bench and still be able to pick them up, but this would not be feasible with a more elevated setup.
3. Lie down with the front of your torso and thighs flat on the bench in a position that allows your arms to hang straight down toward the floor without being obstructed by the objects you used to elevate the bench.
    1. Based on your preference, you can either keep your knees extended or bent.
    2. Position your head in a comfortable position, such as tucking your chin and lifting your head slightly up to keep your face off the bench.
4. Firmly grasp each dumbbell, and angle your palms in toward each other while your arms hang down with straight elbows.
    1. The bony bump on the back of each elbow should be pointing backward, which is the direction in which you will row.
    2. Maintain this neutral grip throughout your set.
    3. To be able to row through a full range of motion, the dumbbells should not be in contact with anything in this position.
5. Grasp each dumbbell, position your arms so that they are perpendicular to the ground with extended elbows, and direct your palms in toward each other to utilize a neutral grip.
    1. To be able to row through a full range of motion, the dumbbells should not be in contact with anything in this position.
## Exercise technique
1. Drive your elbows backward and upward in an arcing motion to pull the dumbbells toward your hip. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blades back together.
    1. Keep your elbows tucked by your sides, rather than flaring them outward.
    2. Progressively bend your elbows to the extent that keeps your forearms close to being perpendicular to the ground throughout the row.
    3. Keep your torso in contact with the bench throughout the set rather than arching your lower back to lift your chest up off the bench.
    4. Pull through your full range of motion, which will typically result in your elbows being level with your torso or slightly behind your torso at the end of the lifting phase.
    5. While performing this arcing motion, the dumbbells should not move straight upward in a vertical path.
    6. As a cue for training your upper back muscles, you can think about squeezing your shoulder blades together at the top of the pull as if you were trying to pinch a pencil with your shoulder blades.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering the dumbbells in a controlled arcing forward motion until your arms are directed down toward the ground with straight elbows.
    1. Allow the weights to pull your shoulder blades apart to stretch your upper back muscles.
    2. You do not have to perform an extremely slow lowering phase, but you should actively control the entire descent rather than letting the weights rapidly pull your arms down.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to set the dumbbells back down.
## Description
While also training your [biceps](../Muscles/Biceps%20Brachii%201b95c6f170d88027b895ed4c0bb40b68.md), [brachialis](../Muscles/Brachialis%2018f5c6f170d880c181eed18b5b379fe9.md), and [brachioradialis](../Muscles/Brachioradialis%201905c6f170d8808f8207e301c4acd3b3.md), this row can effectively develop your [lat](../Muscles/Latissimus%20Dorsi%201905c6f170d880109e73f99ab91d4d47.md), [teres major](../Muscles/Teres%20Major%201905c6f170d880dbba32f6bd22e3abe6.md), [rear delt](../Muscles/Deltoid%20Posterior%20Head%201905c6f170d880a29e5ec26f4d257c01.md), [traps](../Muscles/Trapezius%201b95c6f170d880bebf89c759b592e81f.md), and [rhomboids](../Muscles/Rhomboids%201b95c6f170d880c8a84de94f5232a509.md).

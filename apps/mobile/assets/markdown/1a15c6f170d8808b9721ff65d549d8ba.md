## Setting up
1. Pick up a dumbbell in one hand and sit down on a flat exercise bench with enough room on the bench behind you so that you can lie your head down onto the end of the bench.
    1. If you are using a bench that is built into a rack, you should set up facing toward the base of the rack for sufficient space.
2. Lift the dumbbell up onto your thigh and grasp its handle with both hands as your palms face inward.
3. While simultaneously marching your thigh up to help lift the dumbbell back toward your upper abdomen, lean back to lie down with your back and the back of your head in contact with the bench, as your head is right by the end of the bench.
    1. Once you are lying down, your feet should be on the ground in a stable position with your knees bent, and you will keep them in this position throughout the exercise.
    2. You should now be holding the dumbbell with both hands right above your upper abdomen.
4. Press the weight up until your elbows are straight and your arms are directed straight up toward the ceiling.
5. Reposition your hands so that both of your palms are firmly contacting one end of the dumbbell with your thumbs crossed over each other and your index fingers crossed over each other, which can result in your thumbs and index fingers resembling close to a diamond shape.
    1. In this position, the dumbbell’s handle should be vertical as one end of the weight is below your hands, while the other end is above your hands.
    2. You will continue securely gripping the dumbbell in this manner throughout your set.
## Exercise technique
1. While keeping your elbows fairly straight, lower the weight behind your head in a controlled arcing manner through your available shoulder range of motion.
    1. You should avoid meaningfully bending your elbows, but you can allow them to bend mildly as your arms move through motion at your shoulders while keeping the rest of your body stationary.
    2. Keep your lower back near a neutral position, like when you are normally standing upright, as you avoid excessively arching your lower back during the descent.
    3. People can often lower the weight until their upper arms are close to parallel with the ground, but how far you can descend will be influenced by your shoulder mobility.
    4. Once you feel that you have reached the end range to which your shoulders can safely stretch, you have reached the end range for you as an individual.
2. Reverse the movement by pulling your arms upward and forward in an arcing motion to lift the weight up until you have returned to the starting position with your arms directed straight up toward the ceiling.
    1. Continue keeping your elbows fairly straight as you move your arms through shoulder motion while keeping the rest of your body stationary.
3. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can train your [lats](../Muscles/Latissimus%20Dorsi%201905c6f170d880109e73f99ab91d4d47.md), [teres major](../Muscles/Teres%20Major%201905c6f170d880dbba32f6bd22e3abe6.md), and [rear delt](../Muscles/Deltoid%20Posterior%20Head%201905c6f170d880a29e5ec26f4d257c01.md), which are assisted by the [lowest portion of your pec major](../Muscles/Pectoralis%20Major%20Sternocostal%20Head%201905c6f170d8807d8e58cc809442a60b.md) in the overhead position of each rep.

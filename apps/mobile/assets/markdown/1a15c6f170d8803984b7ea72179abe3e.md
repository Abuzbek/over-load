## Setting up
1. With its heavier side facing forward, firmly grasp an asymmetrical weighted implement (or a viable alternative) by its lighter end at a position that can provide your desired level of resistance.
    1. Asymmetrical weighted implements include adjustable dumbbells with plates loaded only on one side, hammers, and end-loaded baseball bats.
    2. As an alternative to an asymmetrical weighted implement, you can grasp certain symmetrically weighted cylindrical implements closer toward one end, which can provide the same training effect. Examples include a fixed-weight straight bar, a fixed-weight EZ bar, an adjustable EZ bar, and a cable straight bar attachment (like a lat pulldown bar).
    3. The resistance your forearm muscles experience during this exercise will be dictated by how closely to one end you grasp the implement.
    4. For greater resistance, you can grasp farther away from the weighted end with an asymmetrically loaded implement or closer to whichever end you are holding with a symmetrically loaded implement.
    5. When performing this exercise for the first time with a particular implement, you may have to try out different grip positions to determine specifically where you should grasp the implement to provide the magnitude of resistance you wish to have.
2. Hold the implement by your side with your palm facing in toward your side and your elbow straight. Based on your preference, you can stand or sit down with an upright torso.
    1. You have the option of holding the implement with your thumb bent and wrapped around your other fingers, like the standard grip for many exercises. Alternatively, you can situate your thumb flat against the implement as it is pointing forward. While not inherently injurious, certain individuals may experience thumb discomfort in the bottom position of this exercise when using a bent thumb position due to the loaded stretch it provides to a thumb muscle.
## Exercise technique
1. Allow the weight to tilt your hand down through your wrist’s comfortable range of motion to assume the starting position of the exercise.
2. Tilt your hand up toward the thumb-side of your forearm (which is facing forward) through your wrist’s full range of motion.
    1. This will angle your knuckles at least slightly forward.
    2. The heavier part of the implement in front of your thumb and index finger should now be angled at least slightly upward while mostly pointing forward.
    3. Similarly, the lighter part of the implement behind your little finger should now be angled at least slightly downward while mostly pointing backward.
    4. Individuals with normal wrist mobility can typically only tilt their hands mildly beyond a neutral wrist position (like it normally is when standing at rest with your knuckles pointing straight down) during this exercise. Consequently, this exercise does not involve a particularly long range of motion.
    5. While you move your hand through wrist motion, keep the rest of your body stationary throughout the exercise.
3. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing your hand to tilt away from the thumb-side of your forearm through your wrist’s comfortable range of motion.
    1. This will angle your knuckles at least mildly backward.
    2. The heavier part of the implement in front of your thumb and index finger should now be angled diagonally downward and forward.
    3. On the other hand, the lighter part of the implement behind your little finger should now be angled diagonally upward and backward.
    4. Only move through the range of motion that is comfortable to your wrist and thumb.
    5. There is no need to perform this extremely slowly, but you should actively control the movement rather than letting the weight swing your hand down.
4. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively train five forearm muscles, namely the two [extensor carpi radialis muscles](../Muscles/Extensor%20Carpi%20Radialis%20Muscles%201b95c6f170d880b49202f1bf7521fa58.md), [flexor carpi radialis](../Muscles/Flexor%20Carpi%20Radialis%201905c6f170d880bea472f97a5bce0b64.md), [extensor pollicis brevis](../Muscles/Extensor%20Pollicis%20Brevis%201905c6f170d880079d14c018a5133bbd.md), and [abductor pollicis longus](../Muscles/Abductor%20Pollicis%20Longus%201905c6f170d8807b89e7c7ca3c8c19d3.md).

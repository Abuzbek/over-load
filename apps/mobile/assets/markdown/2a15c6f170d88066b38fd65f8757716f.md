## Setting up
1. At a dual cable station, adjust each pulley to a little higher than your head, and select your desired load at the two with stacks.
2. Based on your preference, leave the cables without any handle attachments or connect a D-handle attachment to each cable.
    1. Either option allows you to train your rear delts with essentially the same technique except for the specific grip you will use, so feel free to try out both and use whichever feels most comfortable to you.
3. After standing centered between the cables with your sides facing the weight stacks, take a step backward before side-stepping toward one cable.
4. With your arm that is opposite to the cable, reach across your body and firmly grasp the ball at the end of the cable (if you are not using any handle attachment) or the D-handle (if you are using a handle attachment).
5. Side step toward the other cable, and reach across your body to grasp it with your free hand.
6. Return to being centered between the cables with your arms crossed in front of your torso, with slightly bent elbows, as the pulleys are situated a little in front of you.
    1. In this position, your arms should resemble an “X-shape” with each other as your hands are above shoulder height in line with the resistance from the cables.
    2. Maintain slightly bent elbows throughout your set.
## Exercise technique
1. Sweep your arms out and away from each other while driving them diagonally downward and backward.
    1. Move through the full range of motion your shoulder mobility permits, which should result in your elbows being slightly behind your shoulders or at least horizontally level with your shoulders at the end range.
    2. Strive to achieve a roughly even balance between sweeping horizontally outward and vertically downward.
    3. Keep your torso upright with your chest up throughout the set, while all the motion occurs from your arms moving at your shoulder joints.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing your arms to move diagonally forward, upward, and in toward each other until they have crossed each other and returned to the starting position.
    1. When you set up appropriately, the angle of resistance from the cables will naturally guide your arms through the intended diagonal crossing path.
    2. You do not have to perform this movement with an extremely slow tempo, but you should avoid letting the cables abruptly yank your arms in toward each other.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
    1. After completing your last rep, set the cables back in the opposite way in which you set up initially. Side-step toward one pulley until the cable loses tension, then release it and walk over to the other pulley to do the same.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your [rear delts](../Muscles/Deltoid%20Posterior%20Head%201905c6f170d880a29e5ec26f4d257c01.md) while also training your [traps](../Muscles/Trapezius%201b95c6f170d880bebf89c759b592e81f.md), [rhomboids](../Muscles/Rhomboids%201b95c6f170d880c8a84de94f5232a509.md), [lats](../Muscles/Latissimus%20Dorsi%201905c6f170d880109e73f99ab91d4d47.md), [teres majores](../Muscles/Teres%20Major%201905c6f170d880dbba32f6bd22e3abe6.md), and two [rotator cuff](../Muscles/Rotator%20Cuff%201b95c6f170d880b3b0d8eab69c3a7683.md) muscles to a lesser degree.

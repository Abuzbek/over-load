## Setting up
1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that allows you to rest your torso against the chest pad and enables you to row comfortably with your elbow tucked fairly close to your side.
    1. The appropriate seat height often naturally allows you to finish the lifting phase with your elbow bent by about 90 degrees.
    2. If the seat is too low, the upward angle in which the handle moves during the lifting phase can cause the row to feel awkward as you have to shrug your shoulder up and/or meaningfully flare your elbow out away from your side.
    3. If the seat is too high, you may notice that you have to pack your shoulder blade down to reach the handles with your arm directed further downward than forward. This could limit your range of motion and prompt the row to not feel as natural as it otherwise could.
2. If your machine has an adjustable chest pad, set it to a position that allows you to just grasp the handle when your arm is fully outstretched with a straight elbow.
    1. Machines often lack an adjustable chest pad.
3. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine, and sit down on the seat with your torso against the chest pad.
    1. Based on your preference, you can position your feet against the machine’s foot plates (if your machine model has foot plates), or you can place your feet flat on the floor.
    2. As an alternative to sitting on the seat, you optionally can adopt a staggered stance with one foot forward and the other backward as you lightly contact the seat with your buttocks while predominantly bearing weight through your feet.
    3. Set up in whichever position feels most stable and comfortable to you.
4. Reach forward and firmly grasp the handle with the side you are going to train. If your machine allows you to use different grips, grasp the handle at a spot where you can angle your palm inward to adopt a neutral or semi-neutral grip.
    1. Hold the handle with a straight wrist and elbow.
    2. If your machine has a stationary center handle (that is not connected to either of the machine’s mobile lever arms), you can grasp it with your free hand and continue holding it throughout the set for additional stability. Otherwise, place your free hand wherever you prefer.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull the handle back toward you by driving your elbow backward while bending it. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blade fully back.
    1. Rather than meaningfully flaring it out away from your side, keep your elbow tucked reasonably close to your side to a degree that feels natural and comfortable to you. This should keep your forearm aligned with the direction in which you are rowing.
    2. Pull through your full range of motion, which will typically result in your elbow being slightly behind your torso or at least level with your torso at the end of the lifting phase.
    3. Instead of moving straight backward in a fully horizontal direction, this machine type will normally have its handle move at an upward angle while you pull it backward.
    4. Keep your torso in contact with the chest pad throughout the set.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbow to move forward in a controlled manner as it progressively straightens until your arm is fully outstretched in front of you.
    1. Your shoulder blade will naturally move forward and away from the center of your back, but you can exaggerate this motion by intentionally allowing the weight to pull it further forward than your resting position at the end, which will stretch your upper back muscles.
    2. While you do not have to perform this step extremely slowly, you should avoid letting the weight abruptly yank your arm forward in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.

## Setting up
1. Position yourself with your palms, knees, and toes on the ground as your elbows are straight, and your knees are bent.
    1. In this “on all fours” position, your upper arms and thighs should be directed straight down such that they are approximately perpendicular to the ground.
    2. Your lower back and neck should be near a neutral position with the natural curvature you have when normally standing upright, rather than being rounded or excessively arched.
## Exercise technique
1. Begin bracing your core by taking a deep “belly breath” (also known as a diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward.
    1. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
2. After the deep “belly breath,” you can continue thinking about tensing the front of your abdominals, sides of your abdominals, and lower back muscles simultaneously in a manner that allows you to form a “360 degree brace” instead of only tensing the front of your core.
    1. As a helpful cue for your “360 degree brace,” you can tense the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you are bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forcible slap.
3. While keeping your core braced, sweep one leg backward slowly and in control as you lift it slightly off the ground, straighten your knee, and squeeze your glutes to create whole-body tension that promotes a rigid trunk position.
    1. While moving at your hip, the rest of your body should be as close to stationary as is physically possible for you.
    2. When maintaining an appropriately rigid trunk position, someone should be able to place an object, such as a mug of water, on their back without it falling off as they lift their leg and arm.
    3. Do not lift your leg any higher beyond the range that allows you to keep your trunk in a rigid static position.
    4. Particularly avoid letting your lower back dynamically arch or letting your hip bone (on the side of the leg you are lifting) tilt up.
4. Statically hold your leg in the position you just adopted, then slowly return it to the initial position, with your thighs and upper arms directed straight down toward the ground.
    1. You can aim to hold your leg off the ground for at least a couple of seconds, but it is up to you specifically how long you wish to maintain that position before returning it to the ground.
    2. After returning to the starting position, you can take a breath before resuming to brace your core.
5. Repeat steps 3 and 4 with your opposite leg.
6. Alternate between steps 3 and 5 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can statically train your core muscles to maintain a rigid trunk position as you refine your ability to move through your hips with a stable trunk.

## Setting up
1. Adjust a cable station’s pulley to be near chest height, connect a rope attachment to the cable, and select your weight with the loading pin.
2. While facing the cable station, grasp each end of the rope with your index fingers and adjacent aspects of your hands contacting the flat surfaces of the attachment’s rubber stoppers. Wrap your thumbs over the rounded outer aspects of the stoppers.
    1. If you are using a long strap attachment with looped ends rather than ropes, you naturally will situate your hands within the loops and grasp the inner part of each loop.
3. With your elbows straightened and arms raised to be parallel with the cable, step backward until the machine’s weight plates separate and you feel tension from the cable.
    1. In this position, your palms should be directed down as your arms are outstretched in front of your torso while you are standing upright with your hands close to each other.
4. With your feet near hip width apart, slightly bend your knees, and adopt a staggered stance with one foot mildly in front of the other, or keep your feet symmetrical with each other.
    1. Utilize whichever stance helps you feel the most stable and balanced.
## Exercise technique
1. Drive your elbows backward while flaring them straight out away from your sides to pull the rope toward your shoulders.
    1. While emphasizing driving your elbows back behind you, your elbows will naturally bend without having to focus on pulling with your hands.
    2. Your elbows should finish pointing straight out to your sides as your upper arms are approximately parallel with the ground, forming a “T-shape” with your torso
    3. Finish pulling with your shoulder blades fully pulled back together and your chest up in a “proud chest” posture.
    4. In contrast to a face pull, your hands should remain in front of your shoulders rather than above your shoulders during this high row.
2. Reverse the movement in a controlled manner by allowing your elbows to arc forward and in toward each other while naturally straightening.
    1. Allow your shoulder blades to spread apart naturally from each other as your arms move in line with the cable’s resistance.
    2. You should finish in the starting position with your elbows straightened and arms outstretched in front of you.
    3. You do not have to perform this phase with an extremely slow tempo, but you should avoid letting the cable abruptly yank your arms forward in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This row can effectively develop your [traps](../Muscles/Trapezius%201b95c6f170d880bebf89c759b592e81f.md), [rhomboids](../Muscles/Rhomboids%201b95c6f170d880c8a84de94f5232a509.md), and [rear delts](../Muscles/Deltoid%20Posterior%20Head%201905c6f170d880a29e5ec26f4d257c01.md) while also training your [biceps](../Muscles/Biceps%20Brachii%201b95c6f170d88027b895ed4c0bb40b68.md), [brachialis](../Muscles/Brachialis%2018f5c6f170d880c181eed18b5b379fe9.md), [brachioradialis](../Muscles/Brachioradialis%201905c6f170d8808f8207e301c4acd3b3.md), and three [rotator cuff](../Muscles/Rotator%20Cuff%201b95c6f170d880b3b0d8eab69c3a7683.md) muscles.

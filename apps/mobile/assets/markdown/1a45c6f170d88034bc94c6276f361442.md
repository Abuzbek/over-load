## Setting up
1. Lie down on your side with straight legs, as one leg is resting on top of the other. Align your legs with your torso so that you can draw a straight line from your legs through your hips to your torso when viewed from above.
    1. The outer aspect of your lower foot and lower hip should be resting on the ground.
    2. If you are on a hard surface, you can set up on an exercise mat for comfort. You chiefly want a comfortable surface under the forearm you will place on the ground.
    3. The muscles on the side closest to the ground are predominantly trained during your side plank.
2. Position your bottom forearm on the ground with your elbow bent to about 90 degrees. Your elbow should be close to being stacked directly underneath your shoulder.
    1. With your bottom hand, you can make a fist and keep the little finger-side of your hand in contact with the ground. Alternatively, you can keep your palm flat on the ground if you find that to feel more comfortable and stable.
    2. You can keep your top arm in a comfortable position, such as resting on top of your side with a straight elbow. Alternatively, you can reach that arm toward the ceiling with a straight elbow or place that side’s hand on your top hip with a bent elbow.
    3. Your bottom upper arm should be in a vertical position.
    4. You can perform this step simultaneously with step 1.
## Exercise technique
1. Lift your bottom hip off the ground by pushing through your bottom foot and forearm. You should now be able to draw a straight line from your head through your belly button to the center point between your feet when viewed from the front.
    1. You should also be able to draw a straight line from your heel through your hip to your shoulder when viewed from the top.
    2. Your legs, torso, and head should be in the same alignment as they would be when standing upright with your feet together.
2. Statically hold this position in proper alignment until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your opposite side contacting the ground.
    1. Avoid letting your lower hip sag, which would prevent you from drawing a line through the midline of your body when viewed from the front. If you notice your hip starting to drop down, it can help to think about pushing more forcefully through your bottom foot. When learning this exercise, it can be useful to face a mirror to receive visual feedback if your lower hip begins sagging down.
    2. Avoid letting your buttocks drift backward by bending your hips. This would prevent you from drawing a straight line through your whole body when viewed from above. Squeezing your glutes can help prevent you from unintentionally bending your hips.
## Description
This exercise can statically train your [gluteus medius](../Muscles/Gluteus%20Medius%201925c6f170d880f38e56e316eab6e33b.md), [gluteus minimus](../Muscles/Gluteus%20Minimus%201925c6f170d880468a1ac852b36eeaf8.md), [lateral deltoid](../Muscles/Deltoid%20Lateral%20Head%201905c6f170d8804b9b6ef12253eec774.md), and a variety of core muscles, including your [obliques](../Muscles/Abdominal%20Obliques%201b95c6f170d880f5bdc1c2ca4cd25d5f.md) and [quadratus lumborum](../Muscles/Quadratus%20Lumborum%201965c6f170d880b89d4fc43238472b46.md).

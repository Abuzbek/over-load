## Setting up
1. After loading your desired weight onto the machine, adjust the seat or thigh pad height to a level that allows you to position your thighs snugly against the thigh pad while sitting with your feet flat on the floor.
    1. Some machine models have an adjustable seat, others have an adjustable thigh pad, and some have both.
    2. Depending on the specific model, plate-loaded lat pulldown machines may have the thigh pads positioned so you face toward or away from the weights, and these models often include a chest pad.
    3. The machines in which you sit facing toward the weights may be more likely to have handles angled in a manner that results in an underhand grip being uncomfortable and awkward to use, in contrast to machines where you face away from weights, which often have handles well-suited for an underhand grip.
2. While facing the thigh pads and standing above the seat, firmly grasp the handles with an underhand grip, with your palms facing backward.
    1. The grip width you can use will depend on the specific machine model you use, and you can feel free to select whichever grip width you prefer within the range your machine allows. An approximately shoulder-width grip will often be quite comfortable.
3. While holding the handles, sit down on the machine’s seat with the lower portion of your thighs snugly pressed against the machine’s thigh pad.
    1. When sitting down, allow your elbows to extend in a controlled manner and your arms to raise overhead in line with the machine’s resistance.
    2. If you do not feel tension from the machine in this position (which may occur for certain individuals who are quite tall and have long arms), you may need to lower the seat height to experience resistance throughout the full range of motion for this exercise.
## Exercise technique
1. Drive your elbows down toward your sides while progressively bending them to pull the handles downward.
    1. Simultaneosuly pull your shoulder blades down away from your ears.
    2. Keep your torso in the same position throughout your set rather than progressively leaning backward during the pull.
    3. During the initial portion of the pull, you will drive your elbows downward, but you will drive your elbows simultaneously backward and downward during the second half of the pull.
    4. Pull through your available range of motion, which will typically leave your elbows level with or slightly behind your torso at the end of the pull.
2. Reverse the motion by allowing your elbows to lift upward and away from your torso as they progressively straighten to raise the handles in a controlled manner.
    1. Let your shoulder blades move naturally as they elevate with your arms.
    2. Finish with your arms overhead and your elbows fully straightened.
    3. There is no need to perform this step extremely slowly, but you should avoid letting the machine’s resistance yank your arms up in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can effectively develop your [lat](../Muscles/Latissimus%20Dorsi%201905c6f170d880109e73f99ab91d4d47.md), [teres major](../Muscles/Teres%20Major%201905c6f170d880dbba32f6bd22e3abe6.md), and [rear delt](../Muscles/Deltoid%20Posterior%20Head%201905c6f170d880a29e5ec26f4d257c01.md) while also meaningfully training your [biceps](../Muscles/Biceps%20Brachii%201b95c6f170d88027b895ed4c0bb40b68.md), [brachialis](../Muscles/Brachialis%2018f5c6f170d880c181eed18b5b379fe9.md), and [brachioradialis](../Muscles/Brachioradialis%201905c6f170d8808f8207e301c4acd3b3.md).

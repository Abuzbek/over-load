## Setting up
1. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine, and position the footplates as closely together as the machine permits.
2. If your machine’s knee pads rotate (which is the case for combo machines that can be adjusted to train hip adduction or abduction), position the pads so that they are facing in toward each other.
    1. The knee pads may permanently face in toward each other in machines that exclusively permit you to train hip abduction.
3. If your machine allows you to adjust the backrest angle, position it fully upright.
    1. Most machines have backrests fixed in an upright position.
4. Sit down on the machine's seat with your torso upright against the backrest. Place your feet on the footplates with the outer aspect of your knees and/or lower thighs contacting the knee pads.
    1. If you have rather short legs, you may have to scoot slightly forward for your knees to reach the pads, which will require you to lean your torso somewhat backward to keep your back against the backrest.
## Exercise technique
1. Spread your legs apart from each other through your hips’ available range of motion.
    1. How far apart you can spread your legs will be affected by your hip mobility.
2. Reverse the motion by allowing your legs to move in toward each other in a controlled manner.
    1. After doing so, you should now be in the starting position with your legs as close together as the machine permits.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
While also training several smaller muscles on the side of your hip, this exercise can dynamically train your [glutes](../Muscles/Glutes%201b95c6f170d88002885bca53f30406d5.md), likely with an emphasis on the [upper region](../Muscles/Gluteus%20Maximus%20Upper%20Region%201925c6f170d88070b08aeccda6e5ac88.md) of your gluteus maximus.

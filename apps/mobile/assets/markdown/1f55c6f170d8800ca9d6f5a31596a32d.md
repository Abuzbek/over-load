## Setting up
1. Position yourself on your palms, knees, and toes, with your elbows straight and your knees bent.
    1. In this “on all fours” position, your upper arms and thighs should be directed straight down such that they are approximately perpendicular to the ground.
    2. Your lower back and neck should be near a neutral position with the natural curvature you have when normally standing upright rather than being rounded or excessively arched.
## Exercise technique
1. Begin bracing your core by taking a deep “belly breath” (also known as a diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward.
    1. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
2. After the deep “belly breath,” you can continue thinking about tensing the front of your abdominals, sides of your abdominals, and lower back muscles simultaneously in a manner that allows you to form a “360 degree brace” instead of only tensing the front of your core.
    1. As a helpful cue for your “360 degree brace,” you can tense the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you are bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forceful slap.
3. While keeping your core braced, simultaneously sweep your right leg backward and your left arm forward in a slow, controlled manner, lifting these two limbs slightly off the ground as you straighten your right knee and left elbow.
    1. As you do so, you can squeeze your right glute while squeezing your left hand into a fist to create whole body tension that promotes a rigid trunk position.
    2. While you move your right leg at your hip and left arm at your shoulder, the rest of your body should be as close to stationary as is physically possible for you.
    3. When maintaining the appropriately rigid trunk position, someone should be able to place an object such as a mug of water on top of your back without the object falling off your back as you lift your leg and arm.
    4. Particularly avoid dynamically arching your lower back or tilting your right hip bone up.
    5. Do not lift your leg and arm any higher beyond a range that allows you to keep your trunk in a rigidly static position.
    6. As long as you move an arm and a leg on opposite sides of your body, you can choose which sides you move first in your set, but mentioning the sides as an example can help clarify technique.
4. Statically hold your right leg and left arm in the position you just adopted, then slowly return them to their initial position, with your right knee and left hand on the floor as your upper arms and thighs are directed straight down toward the ground.
    1. You can aim to hold your leg and arm off the ground for at least a couple of seconds, but it is up to you specifically how long you wish to maintain that position before returning them to the ground.
    2. After returning to having both your legs and arms on the ground, take a breath before resuming to brace your core.
5. Proceed to perform steps 3 and 4 with your left leg and right arm in the same manner you just utilized with your other sides.
6. Alternate between steps 3 and 5 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can statically train your core muscles to maintain a rigid trunk position as you refine your ability to move through your hips and shoulders with a stable trunk.

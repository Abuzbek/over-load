While exceptions exist, people often find overhand grip pull-ups to be more difficult on a per-rep basis than chin-ups or neutral grip pull-ups. This discrepancy is most likely influenced by chin-ups and neutral grip pull-ups typically being performed with a technique that emphasizes shoulder extension in the sagittal plane, whereas overhand grip pull-ups are typically performed with relatively more shoulder adduction in the frontal plane. As demonstrated by studies such as those from [Mayer et al. (1994)](Why%20overhand%20grip%20pull-ups%20may%20be%20more%20difficult%20t%202295c6f170d880859b52c49442b23a1e.md) along with [Shklar and Dvir (1995)](../Sources/Isokinetic%20strength%20relationships%20in%20shoulder%20musc%201e85c6f170d880edb356d8402b753008.md), on average, maximal torque for shoulder extension exceeds maximal torque for shoulder adduction. The sample of participants, velocity, and type of muscle action tested can all influence the degree to which torque differs between these two actions, but maximal shoulder adduction torque has been measured to be 63-90% of maximal shoulder extension depending on the specific test conditions. In addition to the effect from how the shoulder moves, the forearm position can also affect how much elbow flexion torque you can generate during your pull. [Guenzkofer et al. (2012)](../Sources/Maximum%20elbow%20joint%20torques%20for%20digital%20human%20mode%201e85c6f170d880aa913bf990fbd539c0.md) found that up to 15% greater elbow flexion torque can be generated when the forearm is in a supinated (used for an underhand grip) or neutral position compared to a pronated (used with an overhand grip) position.

Together, these relationships suggest that most people can generate greater torque at the shoulders and elbows when performing chin-ups or neutral grip pull-ups compared to overhand grip pull-ups, which are therefore likely to be more challenging when performing the same number of reps. Consequently, it is common to be unable to complete as many full range of motion reps of overhand grip pull-ups in a set as with chin-ups or neutral grip pull-ups. Nonetheless, individual differences in factors such as training history and the relative strength of different muscle groups can lead some individuals not to notice differences in difficulty between these variations, or even to find overhand grip pull-ups somewhat easier per repetition.

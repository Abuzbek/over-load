## Setting up
1. Position yourself on your palms, knees, and toes, with your elbows straight and your knees bent.
    1. In this “on all fours” position, your upper arms and thighs should be directed straight down such that they are approximately perpendicular to the ground.
    2. Your lower back and neck should be near a neutral position with the natural curvature you have when normally standing upright rather than being rounded or excessively arched.
## Exercise technique
1. Begin bracing your core by taking a deep “belly breath” (also known as a diaphragmatic breath) that expands the front and sides of your abdominals outward.
    1. Avoid taking a “chest breath” that predominantly lifts your chest upward and leaves your abdominals relaxed or simply sucking your belly button inward.
2. After the deep “belly breath,” you can continue thinking about tensing the front of your abdominals, sides of your abdominals, and lower back muscles simultaneously in a manner that allows you to form a “360 degree brace” instead of only tensing the front of your core.
    1. As a helpful cue for your “360 degree brace,” you can tense the front and sides of your abdominals outward like you would if you are bracing for someone to strike your stomach with a punch or forceful slap.
3. While keeping your core braced, sweep one arm forward slowly and in control, lifting it slightly off the ground as you straighten your elbow and squeeze your hand into a fist to create whole-body tension that promotes a rigid trunk position.
    1. While moving at your shoulder, the rest of your body should be as close to stationary as is physically possible for you.
    2. When maintaining an appropriately rigid trunk position, someone should be able to place an object, such as a mug of water, on your back without it falling off as you lift your arm.
    3. Do not lift your arm any higher beyond the range that allows you to keep your trunk in a rigidly static position.
    4. Particularly avoid letting your trunk rotate to either side, as this would cause your chest to turn to one side rather than keeping it facing straight down toward the ground.
4. Statically hold your arm in the position you just adopted prior to slowly returning it to the initial position with your upper arms directed straight down toward the ground.
    1. You can aim to hold your arm off the ground for at least a couple of seconds, but it is up to you specifically how long you wish to maintain that position before returning it to the ground.
    2. After returning to the starting position, you can take a breath prior to resuming to brace your core.
5. Repeat steps 3 and 4 with your opposite arm.
6. Alternate between steps 3 and 5 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise can statically train your core muscles to maintain a rigid trunk position as you refine your ability to move through your shoulders with a stable trunk.

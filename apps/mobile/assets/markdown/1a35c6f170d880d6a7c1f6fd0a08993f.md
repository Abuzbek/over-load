## Setting up
1. Position a short looped elastic band around both of your thighs.
    1. The band will provide the greatest resistance to your hip muscles if you place it just above your knees, while it will provide less resistance if you place it higher up your thighs.
    2. You can choose where you situate the band on your thighs based on the level of resistance you wish to experience. If you are using a particular band for this exercise for the first time, you can adjust its placement during the set if you wish to change the difficulty after starting the set.
2. Lie down with your back on the ground, and your knees bent at least 90 degrees.
    1. Specifically how much you wish to bend your knees (and correspondingly bring your heels toward your buttocks) is up to your preference and which position feels comfortable to you.
3. Position the soles of your feet flat against each other while spreading your knees apart to bring them closer to the ground.
    1. Spread your knees as far apart as is comfortable to your hips, but there is no need to push them further apart than they would naturally fall during a frog pump without a band. The degree to which your knees will spread out comfortably will be influenced by your hip mobility.
    2. The band exerts a force that will pull your knees together unless you actively push your knees out against the band to assume the appropriate position.
    3. The outer aspect of your feet (on the side of your little toes) should be firmly in contact with the ground.
4. Tuck your chin toward your chest and lift your head slightly off the ground.
    1. This can help you keep your lower back flat rather than excessively arched during the exercise, but you have the option of resting your head on the ground if you wish to do so.
    2. You can position your arms in whichever comfortable position you prefer, such as resting by your sides.
## Exercise technique
1. Lift your hips up off the ground by driving the outer aspects of your feet into the ground and squeezing your glutes. Keep the soles of your feet pressed together and your knees spread apart.
    1. At the end of the lifting phase, you ideally should be able to draw a straight line from your knees through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from the side if you have the mobility to perform the full range of motion. If your hips feel “stuck” or quite uncomfortable before reaching this point, you may not have the hip mobility to move through the full range with your current knee position. Bringing your knees somewhat higher up and closer together may allow you to lift your hips up higher in a more comfortable manner.
    2. Focus on moving your hips and avoid arching your lower back excessively. Tensing your abdominal muscles may help limit your lower back from arching.
    3. If you wish to do so, you can hold the full glute squeeze at the top for a moment, which may assist with feeling your glutes engage during this exercise.
2. Reverse the movement by lowering your hips in a controlled manner until you are able to lightly contact the ground with your buttocks.
    1. Continue keeping your soles in contact with each other.
    2. After determining which knee position allows you to comfortably move through your full range of motion, strive to keep your knees in the same position throughout your set.
3. Alternate between steps 2 and 3 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This exercise dynamically trains your [gluteus maximus](../Muscles/Gluteus%20Maximus%201bb5c6f170d8805380e7c611cd051f9f.md) while statically training your [gluteus medius](../Muscles/Gluteus%20Medius%201925c6f170d880f38e56e316eab6e33b.md), [gluteus minimus](../Muscles/Gluteus%20Minimus%201925c6f170d880468a1ac852b36eeaf8.md), and [short hip external rotator muscles](../Muscles/Short%20Hip%20External%20Rotators%201b95c6f170d880c79035ce63a7c5f540.md).

## Setting up
1. Adjust the machine’s seat to a height that allows you to rest your torso against the chest pad and enables you to row comfortably with your elbows tucked fairly close to your sides.
    1. The appropriate seat height often naturally allows you to finish the lifting phase with your elbows bent by about 90 degrees.
    2. If the seat is too low, the upward angle in which the handles move during the lifting phase can cause the row to feel awkward as you have to shrug your shoulders up and/or meaningfully flare your elbows out away from your sides.
    3. If the seat is too high, you may notice that you have to pack your shoulder blades down to reach the handles with your arms directed further downward than forward. This could limit your range of motion and prompt the row to not feel as natural as it otherwise could.
2. If your machine has an adjustable chest pad, set it to a position that allows you to just grasp the handles when your arms are fully outstretched with straight elbows.
    1. Machines often lack an adjustable chest pad.
3. Add weight plates of your desired load to the machine, and sit down on the seat with your torso against the chest pad.
    1. Based on your preference, you can position your feet against the machine’s foot plates (if your machine model has foot plates), or you can place your feet flat on the floor.
    2. As an alternative to sitting on the seat, you optionally can adopt a staggered stance with one foot forward and the other backward as you lightly contact the seat with your buttocks while predominantly bearing weight through your feet.
    3. Set up in whichever position feels most stable and comfortable to you.
4. Reach forward and firmly grasp the handles. If your machine allows you to use different grips, grasp the handles at a spot where you can angle your palms in toward each other.
    1. Hold the handles with straight wrists and elbows.
    2. If the handles are situated far enough in front of you to make it difficult to grasp both of them simultaneously, it may be easier for you to grasp one handle at a time.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull the handles back toward you by driving your elbows backward while bending them. Simultaneously pull your shoulder blades back together.
    1. Rather than meaningfully flaring them out away from your sides, keep your elbows tucked reasonably close to your sides to a degree that feels natural and comfortable to you. This should keep your forearms aligned with the direction in which you are rowing.
    2. Pull through your full range of motion, which will typically result in your elbows being slightly behind your torso or at least level with your torso at the end of the lifting phase.
    3. Instead of moving straight backward in a fully horizontal direction, this machine type will normally have its handles move at an upward angle while you pull them backward.
    4. Keep your torso in contact with the chest pad throughout the set.
    5. As a cue for training your upper back muscles, you can think about squeezing your shoulder blades together at the end of the pull as if you were trying to pinch a pencil with your shoulder blades.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbows to move forward in a controlled manner as they progressively straighten until your arms are fully outstretched in front of you.
    1. Your shoulder blades will naturally move apart from one another, but you can exaggerate this motion by intentionally allowing the weight to pull them further apart than your resting position at the end, which will stretch your upper back muscles.
    2. While you do not have to perform this step extremely slowly, you should avoid letting the weight abruptly yank your arms forward in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.

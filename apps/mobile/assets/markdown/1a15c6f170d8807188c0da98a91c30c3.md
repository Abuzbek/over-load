## Setting up
1. Select your weight with the loading pin, and adjust the seat to a height that allows your chest to rest comfortably against the chest pad. 
    1. While you can initially adjust the seat to a height that situates your shoulders approximately level with the parallel handles, the seat height that is best for you depends on the specific machine you use, and trying out different positions may be helpful.
2. Adjust the chest pad to a position that allows you to grasp the parallel handles when your arms are fully outstretched in front of you with straight elbows.
    1. To row through your full range of motion, ideally place the chest pad far enough back that you have to push your shoulder blades forward and apart to grasp the handles with your elbows fully straight.
3. Sit down with your chest resting snugly against the chest pad and your feet flat on the floor before reaching fully forward with extended elbows to grasp the parallel handles firmly with your palms facing in toward each other (i.e., a neutral grip).
    1. If you were able to grasp the handles with any amount of elbow bend, you could let go of the handles and adjust the chest pad before grasping the handles again.
## Exercise technique
1. Pull the handles back toward the sides of your torso by driving your elbows backward while simultaneously bending them and pulling your shoulder blades together.
    1. Move your elbows through a path of motion that feels comfortable to you, which will typically involve keeping them tucked close to your sides with this grip rather than notably flaring your elbows away from your sides (which is typically more comfortable with a wider overhand grip).
    2. Keep your chest in contact with the chest pad throughout the set.
    3. Pull through your available range of motion, which will typically result in your elbows being level with your torso or slightly behind your torso at the end of the lifting phase.
    4. As a cue for training your upper back muscles, you can think about squeezing your shoulder blades together at the end of the pull as if you were trying to pinch a pencil with your shoulder blades.
2. Reverse the movement by allowing your elbows to move forward in a controlled manner, progressively straightening until they are outstretched in front of you.
    1. Your shoulder blades will naturally move apart from one another, but you can exaggerate this motion by intentionally allowing the weight to pull them further apart than your resting position at the bottom. This will stretch your upper back muscles and may help you train them more effectively.
    2. While you do not have to perform this step extremely slowly, you should avoid letting the weight abruptly yank your arms forward in an uncontrolled fashion.
3. Alternate between steps 1 and 2 until you have completed your set.
## Description
This row primarily trains your [lat](../Muscles/Latissimus%20Dorsi%201905c6f170d880109e73f99ab91d4d47.md), [teres major](../Muscles/Teres%20Major%201905c6f170d880dbba32f6bd22e3abe6.md), [rear delt](../Muscles/Deltoid%20Posterior%20Head%201905c6f170d880a29e5ec26f4d257c01.md), [traps](../Muscles/Trapezius%201b95c6f170d880bebf89c759b592e81f.md), and [rhomboids](../Muscles/Rhomboids%201b95c6f170d880c8a84de94f5232a509.md), which are assisted by your [biceps](../Muscles/Biceps%20Brachii%201b95c6f170d88027b895ed4c0bb40b68.md), [brachialis](../Muscles/Brachialis%2018f5c6f170d880c181eed18b5b379fe9.md), and [brachioradialis](../Muscles/Brachioradialis%201905c6f170d8808f8207e301c4acd3b3.md).

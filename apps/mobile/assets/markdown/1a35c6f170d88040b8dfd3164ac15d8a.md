## Setting up
1. Adjust the lower leg pad to a position that places it slightly above the back of your heels (i.e., on your Achilles tendons) when lying down on the machine with your knees aligned with the machine’s pivot point.
2. Adjust the machine’s lever arm (that will align with your shins) to a position that allows you to begin each rep with your knees straightened.
    1. If the lever arm is too high, you will start each rep with your knees somewhat bent rather than fully straightened. This would prevent you from training through a full range of motion.
    2. Certain machines may allow you to position the lever arm low enough that it could situate your knees beyond fully straightened into a hyperextended position (where your knees are bent in the opposite direction in which they normally bend). This may increase the likelihood of experiencing knee discomfort during this exercise.
    3. The optimal position will allow you to draw a straight line through your knees from your lower legs to your thighs once you are in the starting position.
3. If your machine allows you to do so, adjust the forearm pad to a height that allows you to rest your forearms on it comfortably without excessively arcing your lower back.
4. Select your desired weight with the machine’s loading pin.
5. Lie down on the body pad with your lower legs under the lower leg pad and your knees aligned with the machine’s pivot point (which will typically result in your knees hanging just off the edge of the body pad).
    1. The front of your thighs, hips, and torso should be in contact with the body pad.
    2. Rather than being excessively arched, your lower back should be near a neutral position with its natural curvature, like when you are normally standing upright.
    3. Your neck should similarly be near a neutral position with its natural curvature, like when you are normally standing upright.
    4. In this starting position, your knees should be straight.
## Exercise technique
1. For additional stability, firmly grasp the handles and continue holding them throughout your set.
    1. If your machine has forearm pads, you should also rest your forearms on them.
2. On the side you are about to train, pull your toes and foot back toward your shin by moving at your ankle. Maintain this ankle position throughout your set.
    1. You should feel the muscles on the front of your shin contract while doing so.
    2. This places your ankle in the same position in which it would be at the bottom of a calf raise on a step. It is the opposite of the ankle motion you would perform when lifting your heel up off the ground during a calf raise.
    3. Holding your ankle in this position can increase your hamstring curl strength (as explained in detail under the “notes” section).
3. While keeping one knee straightened, use your other leg to drive your heel toward your buttock by bending your knee through the full range of motion you can perform. You should typically be able to contact your buttocks or upper thighs with the lower leg pad at the end range.
    1. While your lower leg moves through motion at your one knee, you should keep the rest of your body stationary throughout your set.
    2. Avoid lifting your hips up off the pad or excessively arching your lower back.
4. Reverse the movement as you allow your knee to straighten progressively in a controlled manner until you have returned to the starting position.
    1. You should finish with your knee fully straightened.
    2. Continue keeping your other knee straightened.
    3. While there is no need to use an extremely slow tempo for this movement, you should be actively controlling the weight the entire time, rather than letting the weight abruptly swing your lower leg down.
5. Alternate between steps 3 and 4 until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your other side.
## Description
This exercise can effectively train all your [hamstrings](../Muscles/Hamstrings%201b95c6f170d88055a462c40b686f381a.md), which are assisted by your [gracilis](../Muscles/Gracilis%201925c6f170d880938681fbbe8f075831.md), [sartorius](../Muscles/Sartorius%201925c6f170d8801c8493cac115de0546.md), and [gastrocnemius](../Muscles/Gastrocnemius%201b95c6f170d880b59622dd4a8639aa2f.md).
